About Flexarion: the indie studio behind Vesper Protocol

Flexarion is an independent video game studio founded in 2023 in Aix-en-Provence, France. There are three of us, we love demanding science fiction, and we believe a good game can make computer science fascinating.

Our story

March 2023: a game jam and an old microcontroller

It all started at a 48-hour game jam held in a community hall in Aix-en-Provence. The theme was “lost signal”. Camille turned up with a salvaged circuit board running firmware nobody recognised. Instead of making a shooter, the team decided to turn deciphering that firmware into the heart of the game. The prototype came second in the audience vote — and, more importantly, players stayed glued for an hour to a screen full of hexadecimal.

Autumn 2023: Flexarion is born

Six months later, Léa, Yanis and Camille founded the studio. The name Flexarion blends flexion — the ability to adapt, to bend a system without breaking it — with Orion, the constellation you can see in winter above the Montagne Sainte-Victoire. That sums up our ambition: science-fiction games that ask players to adapt to machines they don't understand yet.

2024 – 2026: Vesper Protocol takes shape

The jam prototype grew into Vesper Protocol. The studio built its own puzzle language based on ternary logic (see our ternary logic tutorial), rewrote its enemy AI three times (read our devlog on the Guardians' AI, in French) and shares every step of production in its development log.

The founding team

Léa Marchetti

Creative direction & game design

A compulsive reader of hard SF, Léa writes the world of the Vesper and tunes puzzle difficulty with a stopwatch. Her rule: “if a player is stuck for more than four minutes, the game is the one at fault”.

Yanis Belkacem

Engine programming & rendering

Yanis looks after the engine, the shaders and performance. He wants Vesper Protocol to run at 60 frames per second on a student's laptop — and he has the benchmarks to prove it.

Camille Roux

Embedded systems & hacking design

A former firmware developer, Camille designs the alien systems you have to hack. Every exploit in the game is based on a real class of vulnerability: buffer overflows, race conditions, hard-coded keys.

Why make video games in Aix-en-Provence?

Aix-en-Provence offers everything a small studio needs: a large community of computer science students, the nearby digital ecosystems of Marseille and the Pays d'Aix, and skies clear enough to look at the stars on the way home from work. Flexarion works partly remotely and meets up several times a week in a coworking space in the city centre.

We take part in local game jams, give talks to students and are always happy to welcome local testers to our playtest sessions. To get in touch, visit the Flexarion contact page (in French).

Our values

  • Accuracy: the science and computing in our games have to hold up.
  • Transparency: we publish our technical choices, mistakes included.
  • Accessibility: you don't need to be an engineer to hack an alien, just curious.

A fictional studio, a real project

Flexarion and its team are invented for a teaching project in the BUT Informatique (computer science degree) at IUT d'Aix-Marseille, France. The website, demos and artwork were made by Adam MANKAI, Gabriel GOUIN and Mattéo YANNI.
